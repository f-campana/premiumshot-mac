What to judge

  • One panel, two sizes. The bottom bar is identical in both. Only the side drawer moves, and the screenshot stays put.
  • Looks are a swatch row. Arrow keys cycle them with a short crossfade. Switch the clipboard between wide, tall and small to see how each shape sits.
  • Copy is explicit and quiet. Return or ⌘C copies. The panel stays open and confirms in place.
  • The editor theme is independent of the look. Flip Panel appearance and the exported image does not change.
  • Inspect the result. Space, the magnifier or a double-click opens it. Swipe up with two fingers to zoom in and down to zoom out (a pinch and + / − work too), then drag to move. Z jumps between fit and 100%, where one output pixel is one screen pixel.
  • Weight compares two presentations. Light has a softer shadow, no desktop dimming and faster motion. Standard is the first version.
  • Read the numbers under the preview. Left: the exact pixels Copy will produce. Right: how big the screenshot itself is drawn compared with its own pixels. It is never enlarged.

Simulated or left open

  • Spacing rule (hypothesis). Margin is 3–19% of the shorter edge (11% by default), enlargement is capped at source pixels.
  • Swipe direction assumes macOS natural scrolling, the default. Safari follows your system setting; in other browsers it cannot be detected.
  • Colour. A browser canvas is sRGB, so the Display P3 question cannot be judged here.
  • Copy tries your real clipboard and the line above reports what happened. Save and drag out are simulated.
  • Notch mode only changes the anchor and where the panel grows from. Hover and expansion behaviour are not modelled.
  • Left out on purpose: redaction, annotations, custom size, adaptive inset. Paste or drop your own image onto the empty panel; it stays in this tab.
